Overview

We introduce RoboFailAtlas, a dataset of complete robot executions collected in RoboTwin and RoboDojo. It preserves successes, failures, retries, and recoveries across diverse manipulation tasks. The RoboFailAtlas-Structured subset links local failure events to atomic actions and visual evidence, while separately recording task progress and terminal success. These annotations support structured reward learning and downstream policy improvement.

Abstract

Failed trajectories capture a broad range of policy behaviors, but large-scale failure data spanning diverse manipulation tasks with fine-grained, event-level annotations remain underexplored. We introduce RoboFailAtlas, containing 124,354 robot execution trajectories across 85 manipulation tasks, and define a taxonomy of 36 failure types, 15 failure outcomes, and three recovery states to describe what goes wrong, which conditions remain unmet, and whether the policy recovers.

We provide a structured subset of 29,500 trajectories across 50 RoboTwin tasks. Each temporally localized failure event is linked to its atomic-action segments and visual evidence, alongside task progress and terminal success. A jointly supervised reward model predicts task completion and structured failure information, enabling fine-grained failure diagnosis and failure-aware policy optimization.

Across five tasks, PPO with structured rewards achieves 50.6% average success, compared with 40.0% for task-only rewards and 40.8% for environment rewards. Further experiments examine failure-guided demonstration selection in simulation and learning from corrective demonstrations on real robots.

Method

Our framework combines complete-trajectory collection, event-level structured annotation, and joint reward learning. The representation separates failure types, unmet task conditions, and recovery states; 70 atomic-action categories supply the execution context.

Structured Annotations

Multimodal annotation drafts are refined through human spot checks, automated verification, and review of flagged cases. Missing or uncertain fields are masked independently. A recovered local failure can coexist with an unsuccessful full trajectory, so recovery and terminal success are recorded separately.

Structured Reward Learning

Given a task instruction and observation history, a Qwen3-VL-based reward model predicts progress, terminal success, failure-associated actions, failure types, outcomes, and recovery states. Its predictions provide task feedback and bounded failure penalties for policy optimization.

Policy Optimization with Structured Rewards

All variants start from the same task-specific SFT policy and are evaluated on the same 100 scenes per task. Structured rewards achieve 50.6% average success, versus 40.8% for environment rewards and 40.0% for task-only rewards.

Failure-Guided Demonstration Selection

Failure-associated actions guide the selection of expert demonstration segments. With matched data amounts and training budgets, failure-guided selection reaches 46.2% average success, compared with 39.6% for random selection.

Real-World Learning from Failure Corrections

On two PiPER tasks, corrective demonstrations start from failure states reached by the initial policy. With matched additional data and training budgets, they improve average success by 7.0 percentage points over adding standard successful demonstrations.
